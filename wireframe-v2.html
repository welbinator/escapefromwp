Common questions

Does this change my live WordPress site?
No. We never log in to your site or touch your server. We only read your public pages the way any visitor would — your live WordPress site keeps running completely untouched the whole time.
Why does the preview look like my site?
We open your site in a real browser and capture everything — your layout, images, fonts, colors. The result looks identical because it's built from exactly what your visitors see today, just running on completely different (and much faster) technology underneath.
Is this actually free?
Yes. The preview is free — no account, no credit card. If you like what you see and want us to actually move your real site and host it, that's $49/month. But the preview costs nothing and doesn't commit you to anything.
What happens after I see the preview?
Nothing automatically. If you want to move forward, use the contact form and we'll handle the full migration — moving your real domain, your content, everything. If you're not ready, the preview just stays there for you to look at.
Will my search rankings survive the move?
Yes. We map every URL from your old site to the same URL on the new one, and set up the proper redirects so Google knows nothing has moved. In practice, most sites improve their rankings after the move because the new site loads significantly faster.

Stolen UpdraftCentral keys are backdooring WordPress sites

Attackers holding valid UpdraftCentral keys are silently installing backdoor plugins on sites running UpdraftPlus — WordPress's most popular backup plugin, with 4M+ installs.

What this means for you

UpdraftPlus is one of the most common WordPress plugins. If you have it installed (and many sites do, for backups), attackers may have had access to your site without you ever knowing. A plugin you installed to protect your site became the way in.

Another emergency PHP security release

The PHP security team issued an urgent patch bulletin — the kind that lands on a regular cadence. WordPress is built on PHP.

What this means for you

Even when WordPress itself is fine, the language it runs on can have holes. A problem with PHP means every WordPress site on that server is potentially exposed — and your host may not patch it immediately.

A critical Elementor flaw let attackers create admin accounts from one link

Elementor 4.3.0/4.3.1 carries a bug rated 8.8/10. If a logged-in admin opens a crafted link, an attacker silently creates a new admin account on your site.

What this means for you
